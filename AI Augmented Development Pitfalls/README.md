# AI Augmented Development Pitfalls

A series about mistakes in AI augmented software development: how they arise,
what they cost, and how to recognize them before they become part of the product.

These pitfalls also occur between people. AI adds another participant that must
interpret everyday language, distinguish intentions from examples, and decide
which existing decisions still apply. The articles examine those judgments
through incremental and iterative development.

1. [When Scope Becomes a Restriction](01-when-scope-becomes-a-restriction.md)
   — A boundary on what we undertake to deliver becomes a requirement that the
   product prevent other behavior.
2. [Removing a Rule Is Not Adding Its Negation](02-removing-a-rule-is-not-adding-its-negation.md)
   — An obsolete rule survives through new assertions about its absence,
   allowing development history to accumulate in tests and documentation.

Each article stands on its own. Both examine how a small amount of useful
product change can acquire a disproportionate amount of implementation,
verification, and explanation. Their examples illustrate the mechanisms;
supporting sources are linked beside the claims they inform. The research
supports the surrounding principles and risks, without establishing the
industry-wide frequency of either specific failure mode.
