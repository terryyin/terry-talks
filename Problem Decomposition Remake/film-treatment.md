# A simple film about problem decomposition

This is a new interpretation of Terry's confirmed argument. It keeps the [full article](../Problem%20Decomposition/problem-decomposition.md) and [original transcript](../Problem%20Decomposition/raw-content.md) as its authority. The earlier films remain separate.

A warm paper field, restrained typography and a single shopping example carry the explanation. Red marks a question or uncertainty; teal marks useful completed value and product health. There are no characters, physical product walls, splashes or stock illustrations. Motion directs attention: questions appear, a result becomes usable, feedback changes the next question, and a few required product parts change while their connections remain coherent.

## Argument and visual evidence

| Section | Visual | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distinction | Screen/API/database belong to an imagined answer; a customer question stands beside them | Organizing a solution and discovering useful answers are different activities |
| Concrete scenario | One item at one store, with opening hours and reservations left for later | A broad need can yield a narrower useful result without planning every internal component |
| Two premises | Smaller useful customer results; a provisional plan | Seek narrower external problems, and recognize uncertain problem solving |
| Goal 1 | “In stock: 1 left” becomes usable, followed by “Did it help?” | Independently useful value enables feedback; just enough, just in time to learn |
| Goal 2 | Completed stock result stays; “The shop was closed” makes opening hours next; reservation remains unstarted | Change direction at completed boundaries, avoiding unfinished waste and product damage |
| Three Vs | Usable result, observable evaluation, completed path through required screen/API/database | Valuable, visible and vertical; not a requirement to touch every component |
| One-piece flow | People work inside one shared customer outcome and finish it | Collaboration serves completion; the customer plan is not component handoffs |
| Smaller scales | Story, scenario and small coherent commits | Repeat the reasoning; every commit serves today's purpose or an existing documented need |
| Whole product | A small number of connected domain components change, the product remains coherent | Narrow customer scope can cross implementation boundaries; health preserves speculative future options |
| Closing | The cover returns | Smaller problems. Useful answers. Freedom to choose again. |

Just-in-time belongs inside the goals. Option value remains secondary and explicitly speculative: future customer value at a given cost, without implementing unused features now. “Every commit is your last commit” is a discipline and an aspiration to useful value, not a guarantee that every commit independently produces customer benefit. The completed-boundary qualifier prevents an absolute promise of free interruption at any instant.

The stock quantity, store, shopper and feedback are fictional explanatory examples, not current commerce data or a claim that this order suits every product. Both problem and solution decomposition remain necessary. The film does not claim that small stories automatically produce good architecture.
