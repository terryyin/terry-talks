# Claim 00: Judgment-intensive work consumes live judgment; judgment-loaded output still demands it

**Status: Provisional — vocabulary and semantic boundaries accepted for the
talk; whether _judgment_ naturally communicates the intended breadth of
intelligence remains open**

## Claim

> **Judgment-intensive work is work in which live, context-sensitive
> intelligence is a material part of reaching the next valid action or
> acceptable result: understanding the situation, framing what matters,
> creating or adapting a response, weighing consequences, and deciding what
> counts as acceptable.**

Its short gloss is **work that still requires live judgment**. _Still_ locates
the work in the current state of learning and capability. It does not make the
work undesirable: the judgment may be the valuable work.

The definition is actor-neutral. A person, an AI capable enough for the
situation, or a human–AI arrangement may supply some or all of the live
intelligence. Capability does not settle authority or accountability. The
talk's five calls that remain human—value, design, credentials, undiagnosed
failure, and ambiguity—are a governance boundary owned by [Claim
6](06-jidoka-embeds-routine-judgment.md), not part of this cognitive
definition.

## The same cognition can play different flow roles

Pulled product work is often planned, while Stop & Fix work emerges from an
abnormality. They are the same cognitive kind when both require this live
intelligence; their flow roles differ.

| Flow role | Why it becomes current work | What live judgment does |
|---|---|---|
| **Pulled product work** | A product need is deliberately selected. | Understand the need and its context; frame what matters; create, adapt, and evaluate a response. |
| **Emergent Stop & Fix work** | A signal or noticed abnormality interrupts the plan. | Understand and contain the abnormality; repair it; learn enough to prevent, detect, or simplify the next occurrence. |

Neither is waste merely because it is judgment-intensive. Product work spends
judgment to create value. A justified stop spends the same kind of judgment to
contain an abnormality before more output inherits it. The stop does not mean
that no work or output follows; it changes what is allowed to flow and makes
the abnormality current work. [Claim 19](19-stop-and-fix.md) owns that halt and
the culture that responds to it.

Both are creation-time problem solving. The relevant contrast is not desirable
versus undesirable judgment; it is live judgment performed as current work
versus a demand for live judgment left in output for someone later.

## Work and output need different names

**Work consumes live judgment.** An artifact or system is
**judgment-loaded** when its use still requires comparable live judgment—for
example, to understand its place in a wider context, determine why it exists,
decide what must remain true, verify whether it is honest and consistent, or
work out how it may safely change.

Judgment-loaded does not by itself mean bad. A proposal, prototype, novel
analysis, or intentionally human-operated system may appropriately remain
judgment-loaded. The failure is treating an unowned or unverified artifact as
finished while leaving later people to **interpret, rank, and re-decide**. If
such artifacts are produced faster than people can understand, verify, own,
and integrate them, unfinished work accumulates as **judgment-stacked
inventory**.

Use the terms according to what they describe:

- **judgment-intensive** describes work or an activity that consumes live
  judgment now;
- **judgment-loaded** describes an artifact or system whose use still demands
  live judgment;
- **judgment-preserved** describes earlier judgment carried by a closed,
  owned mechanism; and
- **judgment-removed** describes a design in which the recurring question no
  longer arises.

Do not call code, documents, analyses, or candidate solutions
_judgment-intensive_. Name them judgment-loaded when the demand is in their
use, and name the action when useful: judgment is required to understand,
verify, accept, integrate, operate, or change them.

## Smart → dumb → gone

Pair the memorable captions with their precise names:

| Caption | Precise name | What later use requires |
|---|---|---|
| **smart** | **judgment-loaded** | Live intelligence: interpret, rank, re-decide. |
| **dumb** | **judgment-preserved** | A closed mechanism carries earlier judgment. |
| **gone** | **judgment-removed** | The recurring question or failure-producing path no longer exists. |

The captions describe the demand left **in use**, not the intelligence that
created the result. A dumb detector or a design that makes the question gone
may require substantial judgment-intensive work to create. [Claim
6](06-jidoka-embeds-routine-judgment.md) owns this descent and the jidoka
mechanism that embeds previously learned judgment in a closed question.

The resulting terminology is:

> **Work consumes live judgment.**  
> **Artifacts and systems are judgment-loaded, judgment-preserving, or
> judgment-removing.**  
> **Unfinished artifacts accumulate as judgment-stacked inventory.**  
> **Human-triggered jidoka spends judgment to stop rather than letting that
> inventory accumulate.**

## Implication for the talk

Introduce the concept through the short gloss—**work that still requires live
judgment**—before using the compact term. Show valuable pulled product work and
emergent Stop & Fix work as two flow roles for the same cognitive resource.
Locate the failure elsewhere: in judgment-loaded output passed on as finished,
forcing someone later to reconstruct the context and do the real judging.

Then use the paired descent consistently:

> **smart / judgment-loaded → dumb / judgment-preserved → gone /
> judgment-removed**

## Question still open

- Does **judgment** naturally communicate the full intended idea to the
  audience: high-level intelligence that can understand context, frame what
  matters, create or adapt a response, evaluate consequences, and decide what
  is acceptable? _Judgment_ can be heard more narrowly as evaluation or final
  decision. Keep **judgment-intensive work** as the current term, preserve the
  broader definition explicitly, and revisit it if audience testing shows that
  listeners miss its contextual or creative dimensions.
