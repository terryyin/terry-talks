# Claim 7: The Algorithm has a TPS family resemblance; it is not a proven extension of TPS

**Status: Provisional — one sequence, family resemblance, not a proven
TPS extension; staging label and McNeill full-text still open**

## Claim

> **Musk and McNeill present the same Tesla/SpaceX five-step sequence,
> not two related systems. Its operating logic resembles TPS and lean.
> Direct derivation from TPS is unproven. The Algorithm is a portable
> order of attack, not a complete extension of TPS.**

Musk explained the five-step engineering process on the 2021 Everyday
Astronaut Starbase tour. Isaacson's 2023 biography published it as “the
algorithm.” McNeill's 2026 book presents it as Elon's five-step
framework, distilled by Tesla's leadership team, used weekly at Tesla
and SpaceX, and brought together after the Model 3 production
crisis—when the company automated first and almost failed.

McNeill's printed labels and Musk's 2021 spoken labels are the same
sequence:

| | McNeill | Musk (2021) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Question every requirement. | Make the requirements less dumb. |
| 2 | Delete every possible step in the process. | Try very hard to delete the part or process. |
| 3 | Simplify and optimize. | Simplify or optimize the design. |
| 4 | Accelerate cycle time. | Accelerate cycle time. |
| 5 | Automate. | Automate. |

Musk adds operating tests: a named person behind every requirement; if
deleted parts never return, deletion was not aggressive enough; do not
accelerate or automate before the first three steps. In interviews
McNeill sometimes groups delete with simplify, then optimize. The
published sequence is still Musk's five.

### Family resemblance, not proven lineage

The correspondence with lean process reasoning is real: start from
customer value, eliminate non-value steps rather than improving them,
make remaining work capable before adding machinery, shorten response
time, automate last. Toyota currently says not to begin with a machine:
learn the work by hand, apply kaizen, then mechanize with abnormality
detection. That last match is the strongest, and is already the jidoka
lesson in
[Claim 6](06-jidoka-embeds-routine-judgment.md). Womack and Jones's five
lean principles sit in the same family: define customer value, identify
the value stream, eliminate non-value steps, place remaining work in
flow and pull, and pursue perfection. Question → delete → simplify →
accelerate is that order of attack without pull or perfection as named
steps.

McNeill says he entered Tesla as a student of TPS and lean. His
practical advice includes going to the front line and deleting steps
the customer does not pay for. Those are not accidental similarities in
vocabulary.

That is **shared operating logic**. It is not **shared intellectual
genealogy**. McNeill's TPS background is documented. The framework's
reported origin is Tesla's own production experience. No primary source
found so far has Musk or another Tesla leader naming TPS, lean, Ohno, or
Toyota as the source of the five steps.

### Narrower than TPS

McNeill does not present The Algorithm as TPS extended. He says TPS and
lean are excellent for incremental improvement and insufficient for
“quantum growth.” The publisher markets it as lean's **antithesis**.

That contrast understates TPS. Toyota describes complete waste
elimination, rapid fulfillment of customer need, challenge of new
methods, and continuous evolution—not only local optimization. Lean's
instruction to challenge every step can produce a radical redesign.

The five labels do not by themselves express customer pull and limits on
work in process
([Claim 4](04-jit-assurance-resourcefulness-and-constraint.md));
jidoka's detect, stop, contain, and prevent
([Claims 3](03-jidoka-enables-jit-trusts-respect-grows.md) and
[6](06-jidoka-embeds-routine-judgment.md)); standards and learning loops
([Claim 18](18-continuous-improvement-towards-perfection.md)); respect
and capability development
([Claim 12](12-respect-for-people-who-can-think.md)); or coordination of
the whole production system.

Questioning and deletion can remove waste or remove safeguards. TPS
supplies the criteria for whether speed is dependable capability.

## Implication for the talk

The Algorithm is a useful bridge from a contemporary technology
audience back to TPS. Its memorable surface is:

> **Question → delete → simplify → accelerate → automate.**

TPS adds the system questions that make that sequence trustworthy:

> **Value for whom? Pulled by what need? What exposes and stops
> abnormality? Who has authority to respond? What learning is
> preserved?**

For AI-augmented software, introducing AI first as automation or
acceleration can generate more of a requirement, process, design, or
code path that should not exist. Before scaling generation:

1. Is the requirement real, current, and attributable to a person or
   need?
2. Can the feature, handoff, approval, artifact, or code be deleted?
3. Can the remaining problem and design be simplified?
4. Can the path to trustworthy feedback be shortened?
5. Which part should now be automated or AI-assisted, with what stop
   signal?

The fifth question is the jidoka qualification from
[Claim 6](06-jidoka-embeds-routine-judgment.md). “Automate last” is not
enough. The automated system must know what evidence matters, when to
stop, how to contain bad output, and when accountable human judgment is
required.

> **The Algorithm shows a prominent AI-era industrial practice using a
> lean-compatible order of attack: remove needless work before making
> work faster. TPS shows what the five-step slogan leaves out: pull,
> built-in quality, human agency, and continuous learning are what make
> speed safe and sustainable.**

## Questions still open

- Does the full text of McNeill's book describe The Algorithm as derived
  from, opposed to, or complementary with TPS beyond the publisher's
  framing and his interviews?
- Is there a primary statement from Musk or another Tesla leader naming
  TPS, lean, Taiichi Ohno, or Toyota as a source for the five-step
  sequence?
- On stage, call it a **radicalized lean sequence**, a
  **lean-compatible counterpoint**, or a **partial family
  resemblance**—and how hard to push that McNeill's
  incremental-versus-quantum contrast understates TPS?
- What empirical evidence would distinguish genuine cycle-time
  improvement from speed obtained by shifting quality, safety, or human
  costs downstream?
- When AI can assist questioning, deletion, and simplification as well
  as automation, is the five-step order still a sequence of tools or a
  sequence of commitments?
