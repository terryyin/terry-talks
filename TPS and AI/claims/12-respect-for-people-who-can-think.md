# Claim 12: Respect for People cultivates people who can think

**Status: Provisional — people-as-thinkers thesis settled; whether the
talk lists four software skills is still an editorial choice**

## Claim

> **Respect for People treats people as capable of taking
> responsibility, thinking for themselves, and improving the work.
> Jidoka and JIT depend on that capability; real problems, teamwork,
> kaizen, and teaching help grow it.**

TPS is not a system that merely uses people's labor. It gives people
responsibility for running, stopping, and improving the work, and it
relies on their developing judgment. “Respect for People cultivates
people who can think” is Terry's synthesis of the sources below, not a
verbatim Toyota definition.

Toyota Way 2001 places **Respect** and **Teamwork** under Respect for
People:

- **Respect** means understanding others, taking responsibility, and
  building mutual trust.
- **Teamwork** includes personal and professional growth, shared
  development opportunities, and stronger individual and team
  performance.

Fujio Cho connects this philosophy directly to capability: Toyota
cultivates people who can think on their own and improve the
workplace—part of what it means by “making things means making people.”
Toyota's current account of TPS likewise says that human wisdom and
ingenuity are indispensable and that it must develop people who can
think independently and implement kaizen.

This is not a later HR layer placed beside the production system. A
1977 Toyota-authored paper described a **respect-for-human system** in
which workers display their capabilities by participating in running
and improving their own workshops. Stop authority, local decisions, and
responsibility for improvement make people participants in the system
rather than labor to be utilized.

> **Respect and Teamwork provide the trust, responsibility, challenge,
> and support. TPS gives people real problems to solve. Together they
> develop the capability on which jidoka, JIT, and kaizen depend.**

## A downturn is evidence, not the definition

Employment practice can reveal whether an organization regards people
as capability or merely as variable labor, but a no-layoff rule is not
Toyota's definition of Respect for People.

Toyota's record also needs qualification. The 1950 crisis included
layoffs and wage cuts; the rupture helped lead to the 1962 Joint
Declaration of Labor and Management. In later downturns Toyota often
protected regular employees and redirected non-production time toward
training and improvement, but this did not mean everyone was retained
on full pay: temporary and seasonal labor, overtime, hiring, wages, and
working time could still absorb reductions.

> **Do not create inventory merely to keep people busy. Preserve and
> develop capability where possible; use slack for learning and
> improvement.**

[Claim 8](08-technical-excellence-enables-jit-coordination-in-less.md)
owns the paired argument against overproduction. [Claim
4](04-jit-assurance-resourcefulness-not-abundance.md) owns the longer
historical account.

## Translation to software and AI

The sourced skills are **problem-solving** and **teaching others to
solve problems**. Analysis is part of genchi genbutsu and kaizen;
collaboration belongs under Teamwork; coaching is later Lean, LeSS, and
Kata language. Problem-solving, collaboration, and teaching may be
enough on stage; a four-skill software list is Terry's vocabulary, not
Toyota's.

For AI-augmented software development, the test is not whether people
are treated kindly while models generate more output. It is whether the
work still develops people who can judge usefulness, notice and stop
harm, improve the system, collaborate on the whole product, and teach
others.

Using AI to bypass all of that thinking consumes the capability on
which the system depends. That is the bottleneck [Claim
6](06-jidoka-embeds-routine-judgment.md) names. Saved time should become
capacity for review, learning, and kaizen—not a reason to generate a
second pile of work or to conclude that the people are no longer
needed. Claim 6 owns judgment descent; [Claim
10](10-freedom-and-trust-reinforce-through-jidoka.md) owns deskilling.

## Implication for the talk

> **Cultivate people who can think for themselves and improve the
> workplace. Making things also means making people.**

Then connect it to [Claim
3](03-jidoka-enables-jit-trusts-respect-grows.md):

> **Jidoka frees attention. JIT entrusts people with responding to real
> need. Respect and Teamwork help them grow through responsibility,
> real problems, collaboration, kaizen, and teaching.**

## Questions still open

Whether the talk needs a four-skill software list at all, or whether
problem-solving, collaboration, and teaching are enough.
