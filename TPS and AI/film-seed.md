# TPS and AI film: Freedom and Trust on mobile

## Parent problem and desired effect

For **Terry**, the bilingual *Freedom and Trust* presentation contains an
argument he wants to share beyond the conference. **Developers and product
people watching on a phone** should grasp its most important message through
a coherent film of around 90 seconds, understandable with English subtitles
and sound off. This advances the backlog's direction through a finished film
grounded in Terry's own material.

### I can share the core message of my TPS and AI talk in a 90-second English film

- **For / why:** Terry can share the talk's essential idea with mobile viewers
  who have not seen the presentation, without asking them to watch the whole
  talk or read a condensed deck.
- **Research and editorial work:** Study the current presentation, including
  its speaker notes, the main-theme document, and the claim research and talk
  roles. Establish what the presentation is most trying to deliver, distinguish
  the central argument from supporting examples, and retain source links for
  the chosen message and film beats. Check primary sources where an included
  factual claim needs verification; research supports Terry's argument.
- **Starting interpretation to validate:** *Freedom and Trust*: judge AI by
  whether teams become more freed than constrained by what they build. Encode
  learned judgment in simple rules, stops, and prevention so people can learn;
  pull small slices from real customer needs and trust people with the next
  real problem. AI speeds whichever loop it feeds. This is an editorial
  starting point from the deck's diagnostic, takeaways, and closing, rather
  than a settled script or a requirement to squeeze every takeaway into it.
- **Presentation theme and material:** Adapt the deck's visual theme, artwork,
  metaphors, and relevant examples into a film with a clear opening, developing
  argument, and memorable ending. Preserve its recognizable identity while
  composing shots for a square frame and mobile reading. TPS supplies reasoning
  for software development; preserve the talk's qualifications and attribution
  when using its research and imagery.
- **Format and language:** Around **90 seconds**, **square (1:1)**, and
  **English only** in audience-facing titles, captions, graphics, and any
  narration. The source deck is Japanese and English; adapt bilingual assets
  where necessary so Japanese text does not remain in the English film.
  Terry will make the Japanese translation separately.
- **Subtitles and mobile viewing:** English subtitles carry the complete
  message with sound off. Compose and pace them for phone viewing, with clear
  contrast, comfortable reading time, and space that avoids covering essential
  action or text. Keep the English caption source available for Terry's later
  translation.
- **Delivery:** A watchable video export, editable film inputs and reused or
  adapted assets in Terry Moves, and the source-linked editorial rationale.
- **Evaluation:** Terry recognizes the presentation's intended message and
  theme in the film. A viewer unfamiliar with the talk watches it on a phone
  with sound off and can explain its central lesson for AI-assisted development.
  Check the export's square dimensions and approximate 90-second duration,
  English-only content, and subtitle readability through the whole film.
- **Depends on:** No unfinished queued story is a prerequisite; the existing
  presentation and research supply the source material.
- **Safe stopping point:** The complete English film and its editable source
  remain usable independently of later translations or other filmmaking work.

This records the requested story for later refinement and planning. It does
not start film production. Japanese translation is outside this story.
